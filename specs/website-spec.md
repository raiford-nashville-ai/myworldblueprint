## The Tool Layout Map

**Slogan:** *Every Tool knows how it wishes to be experienced.*

**Intent:** To establish a self-describing visual and experiential map through which every Tool guides AI in generating its unique interface without prescribing technical implementation.

### The 7 Layout Dimensions

1. **Workspace:** The Tool's dedicated right-hand content pane, revealed when selected from the persistent left sidebar.
2. **Visual Essence:** The atmosphere, personality, and aesthetic character of the Tool.
3. **Layout Map:** The natural arrangement, hierarchy, and flow of interface elements.
4. **Primary Focus:** The central interaction that commands the Builder's attention.
5. **Input Experience:** How the Builder expresses intention, from a simple text field to a sophisticated form.
6. **Action & Feedback:** How the Builder initiates creation and experiences progress, responsiveness, and transformation.
7. **Output Experience:** How results emerge, organize themselves, and invite further exploration.

### The Universal Layout Principle

**Tool Identity → Visual Essence → Layout Map → Interaction → Feedback → Manifestation**

Every Tool carries its own Layout Map as part of its self-description. The map communicates **what the Builder should see, feel, and experience**, giving AI sufficient semantic guidance to generate an appropriate interface.

A Tool may manifest as a single input and button, a structured form, an interactive canvas, or a complex creative workspace. Its complexity emerges from its purpose, not from a universal interface template.

### The Guiding Principle

**The portal provides consistency. The Tool provides its own expression. The Builder experiences one coherent creative environment.**

The Layout Map describes the intended experience, not the code. AI translates that intention into a functional interface while preserving the Tool's identity, purpose, and relationship with the Builder.

## What Is a Tool?

**Slogan:** *One purpose. One transformation. Infinite creative potential.*

**Intent:** To establish the Tool as a first-class, self-describing primitive that transforms creative friction into meaningful outcomes while understanding and enriching the Builder's experience.

### The 9 Essential Attributes

1. **Name:** The Tool's canonical identity.
2. **Description:** What the Tool does.
3. **Purpose:** Why the Tool exists.
4. **Intent:** What the Tool seeks to accomplish.
5. **Motto:** Its essence expressed as a memorable promise.
6. **Input:** What the Builder provides.
7. **Output:** What the Tool manifests.
8. **Friction:** The creative obstacle it addresses.
9. **Resolution:** The transformation that removes the obstacle.

### The Builder Experience

**The Builder** is the creative mind encountering the Tool, bringing intentions, aspirations, uncertainties, and an evolving experiential state.

Every Tool understands its relationship with the Builder through six dimensions:

1. **Audience:** The creative minds the Tool is designed to serve.
2. **Use Cases:** The situations, intentions, and scenarios that bring the Builder to the Tool.
3. **Before:** The Builder's initial state of being, including needs, emotions, and creative friction.
4. **During:** The felt experience of interacting with the Tool, including discovery, agency, clarity, and creative momentum.
5. **Remedy:** The meaningful transformation that resolves the Builder's underlying need.
6. **After:** The Builder's resulting experiential state, including relief, confidence, inspiration, fulfillment, or renewed possibility.

### The Universal Tool Principle

**Friction → Intention → Interaction → Transformation → Resolution → Enrichment**

Every Tool manifests as a focused workspace within the website's persistent sidebar. Its interface minimizes complexity while its intelligence facilitates a meaningful transformation.

Tools are modular, extensible, and composable. One Tool's output may become another Tool's input, creating an interconnected ecosystem of creative possibilities.

### The Builder–Tool Relationship

**Before → During → Remedy → After**

A Tool's success is measured not only by what it produces, but by what changes for the Builder.

**The Builder arrives with a need, participates in creation, receives a remedy, and leaves in an enriched state of being.**
