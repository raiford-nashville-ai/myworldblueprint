THE WORKSHOP / WORLD BUILDER

Start with a spark.

What world do you want to create?

A floating city, a neighborhood full of secrets, a civilization beneath the sea—or the inner world of a single idea. Start with what draws you in: who lives there, what makes it different, or how it feels. A few sentences are enough.

More tips for bringing your world into focus

You don’t need a finished story or answers to everything. Pick a few of these prompts and leave the rest open.

  • The seed: What if something familiar worked differently? What question, image, or possibility keeps pulling at you?
  • The scale: A room, a village, a planet, an imagined society, or the world inside a memory. Small worlds count.
  • The inhabitants: Who or what belongs here? What do they need, believe, value, or misunderstand?
  • The rules: What is possible here—and what has a cost? Consider nature, magic, technology, customs, or unusual laws.
  • The connections: Who depends on whom? What binds people, places, and ideas together? Where do they clash?
  • The feeling: Cozy, uncanny, hopeful, ancient, restless? Add a sound, a texture, or a detail someone would notice on arrival.
  • The change: What happened before, what is happening now, or what is about to disturb the ordinary?
  • The boundaries: What must remain true? What should stay out? Where would you welcome a surprise?

“A coastal town where the tide returns lost memories. The lighthouse keeper decides which ones reach the shore. It feels gentle but uneasy; nobody can recover a memory without giving one away.”

Try: “A world where… Its inhabitants… The one rule is… It feels like… I want to explore…”

Fragments, contradictions, and unanswered questions are welcome. You’re planting a seed, not writing an encyclopedia.
